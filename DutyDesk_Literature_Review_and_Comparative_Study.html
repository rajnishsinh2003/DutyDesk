1. Introduction

Conducting examinations at universities, autonomous colleges, and statutory testing boards involves numerous parallel, high-stakes operational activities: allocating invigilators equitably across halls, securing confidential question papers during transit and unsealing, verifying staff physical attendance, preventing student malpractice, counting answer scripts, and calculating invigilator honoraria. In prevailing institutions, these are handled through separate, manual processes such as spreadsheet rosters, paper sign-in registers, phone calls, and unencrypted WhatsApp groups. This creates severe structural failure modes:

  • Unfair or Clashing Duty Allocation: Disproportionate duty loads, departmental schedule conflicts, and lack of transparency.
  • Late Arrivals and No-Shows: Tardiness and absences are detected only minutes before exam start, leaving exam halls unattended.
  • Weak Chain of Custody for Question Papers: Paper packets lack verifiable custody timestamps and proof of when envelopes are cut open.
  • Script Counting Errors & Missing Answer Sheets: Discrepancies between present candidates and collected scripts surface days later at evaluation centers.
  • No Tamper-Proof Record: In the event of paper leakage allegations, cheating controversies, or administrative grievances, institutions lack tamper-evident digital records.

DutyDesk is an enterprise platform built with Flutter and Firebase that digitizes this entire workflow with role-based access, geofenced attendance, standby auto-promotion, paper dispatch tracking, answer script reconciliation, and immutable audit logging.

2. Problem Statement

Existing software tools address isolated slices of academic administration: generic commercial shift scheduling (Deputy, When I Work), university course timetabling (FET, aSc), or remote computer-based test proctoring (ProctorU, Mercer Mettl).

Core Problem Statement: No single affordable, open-source system integrates duty allocation, attendance verification, paper security, answer script reconciliation, and compliance audit for physical, offline examinations in the Indian institutional context.

3. Objectives

  • Automate fair invigilation duty allocation with deterministic clash detection.
  • Verify invigilator physical presence using GPS, BLE beacons, dynamic QR Gate Passes, and camera face verification.
  • Provide a tamper-evident chain-of-custody workflow for question papers from vault checkout to room unsealing.
  • Reconcile answer scripts mathematically and generate printable vector Form B dockets.
  • Maintain an immutable, auditable log of all administrative mutations.
  • Support English, Hindi, and Gujarati, and operate resiliently under poor or disconnected network conditions.
  • Provide role-specific dashboards for Admin, Invigilator, COE, Vigilance, Dean, Statutory Auditor, and Students/Parents.

4. System Overview & Key Capabilities

DutyDesk comprises 39 modules built on Flutter (Material 3) with Riverpod unidirectional state management, GoRouter navigation, Firebase Auth for role-based access, and Cloud Firestore as the real-time database. Supporting services include Geolocator (geofencing), BLE beacon detection, Camera (face verification), Flutter TTS, PDF/Excel generation, SMTP, SMS/WhatsApp gateways (Msg91, Fast2SMS, Twilio), and FCM push notifications.

5. Literature Review

5.1 Staff Scheduling and Rostering Theory

Personnel scheduling is a long-studied problem in Operations Research. Ernst et al. (2004) and Van den Bergh et al. (2013) reviewed applications, models, and solution methods including integer programming, constraint programming, metaheuristics (genetic algorithms, simulated annealing, tabu search), and rule-based heuristics. Burke et al. (2004) established in nurse rostering that human scheduling involves non-negotiable hard constraints (no double-booking, certified qualifications, approved leave) and flexible soft constraints (fairness, shift preferences). Practical enterprise systems favor fairness-based greedy heuristics paired with manual override capabilities rather than brittle exact solvers.

5.2 Examination Timetabling vs. Invigilator Allocation

Examination timetabling literature (Carter, Laporte and Lee, 1996; Qu et al., 2009) concentrates on assigning course exams to time slots and rooms to eliminate student schedule clashes. Invigilator operational allocation and exam-day ground logistics are comparatively neglected in academic literature. This represents a significant research gap that DutyDesk directly addresses.

5.3 Fairness in Workload Distribution

Fair distribution of duties reduces faculty dissatisfaction and organizational conflict. Rostering research treats fairness as equalizing cumulative workload, undesirable duties, and consecutive assignments. DutyDesk's fairness algorithm, availability calendar, and peer duty swap system embody these principles.

5.4 Location-Based Attendance and Geofencing

GPS-based attendance is common in workforce management. Known limitations include indoor GPS drift, mock-location spoofing, and poor satellite signal penetration inside concrete buildings. Combining multiple physical signals (GPS Haversine, BLE beacons, dynamic QR Gate Passes, and camera verification) provides defense-in-depth presence assurance.

5.5 Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

Sandhu et al. (1996) formalized RBAC, where permissions attach to organizational roles rather than individuals. DutyDesk applies RBAC through Firebase Authentication tokens and Cloud Firestore security rules across eight distinct administrative tiers.

5.6 Chain of Custody and Tamper Evidence

Forensic chain-of-custody concepts (documented handover, exact timestamps, witnesses, physical seals) apply directly to confidential examination material. Question paper leaks represent a recurring crisis in public examinations in India. Time-locked unsealing, dual student witness verification, and tamper incident filing implement these principles.

5.7 Audit Logging and Accountability

Audit trails support accountability, non-repudiation, and compliance. Industry best practice records who, what, when, and from where, protecting logs from modification.

5.8 Offline-First Design

Many university campuses and rural test centers have unreliable internet. An offline-first design (local SQLite queue with automatic synchronization upon reconnection) ensures zero operational disruption.

5.9 Multichannel Notification

In India, SMS and WhatsApp channels achieve significantly higher real-time open rates than email. DutyDesk integrates FCM push notifications, SMS gateways, WhatsApp webhooks, and TTS voice alerts.

5.10 Cross-Platform and Serverless Development

Flutter provides a single codebase for Android, iOS, and Web, while Firebase supplies managed authentication, real-time data, and messaging. This drastically reduces infrastructure costs for institutions with lean IT teams.

8. Gap Analysis

  • No Integrated Solution: Existing systems cover one slice; DutyDesk covers the full physical exam-day lifecycle.
  • Physical Exams are Neglected: Edtech prioritizes online exams, while over 85% of exams in Indian universities are pen-and-paper.
  • Weak Attendance Integrity: Single-signal GPS can be spoofed; DutyDesk layers GPS, BLE, QR Gate Passes, and camera checks.
  • No Automated No-Show Recovery: DutyDesk promotes standby staff automatically upon grace-timer expiration.
  • Paper Security is Largely Manual: DutyDesk digitizes custody, time-locking, and student witness verification.
  • Language and Cost Barriers: DutyDesk is completely free, open-source, and supports English, Hindi, and Gujarati.
  • Compliance Gap: Statutory auditors and flying squads lack dedicated digital tools in prevailing institutional setups.

9. Unique Contributions of DutyDesk

  1. A single unified platform covering allocation, attendance, paper custody, script reconciliation, and audit.
  2. Multi-factor presence verification (GPS + BLE + QR Gate Pass + Face verification).
  3. Automatic standby promotion with grace timers.
  4. Time-locked paper unsealing requiring two student witnesses.
  5. Mathematical script reconciliation with auto-generated Form B docket.
  6. Role variants for COE, Flying Squad, and Dean, plus an auditor observer mode.
  7. Regional language support (English, Hindi, Gujarati) and offline-first operation.
  8. Open source with zero licensing cost (MIT license).

11. Limitations to Acknowledge (Viva Defense)

  • Facial Verification vs. Matching: Face verification with a camera preview and oval frame verifies presence and liveness; it is not automated 1:N neural network matching against a pre-stored biometric template database.
  • Audit Immutability: Immutability in Firestore depends on security rules blocking updates and deletes; a cryptographic hash chain would provide mathematical non-repudiation.
  • Geofencing: Can still be affected by root-level GPS spoofing unless OS mock-location detection APIs are enforced.
  • CCTV Streaming: Evaluated against standard network RTSP/HLS IP camera streams rather than air-gapped proprietary campus DVR hardware.
  • Scalability: For large-scale multi-campus deployments (>10,000 concurrent users), Firestore read costs require aggressive SQLite caching and edge sync.

12. Conclusion

Existing systems fall into scheduling tools, ERPs, online proctoring, and manual methods, and none provides integrated, secure, auditable management of physical examination operations. DutyDesk fills this gap with a multi-role, multilingual, offline-capable, and open-source platform. With the proposed enhancements (explainable fairness, hash-chained audit, anomaly detection), it establishes substantial practical and academic research value.
